01 · The problem

Follow-ups slip when the front desk is busy

Small clinics get inquiries from Facebook, their website and walk-ins. The front desk is also checking in patients, answering calls and handling payments. Follow-ups happen late, or not at all.

Every inquiry that doesn't get a reply is a patient who may book somewhere else.

The goal: log every inquiry in one place, and make sure every inquiry gets a reply and two reminders without anyone doing it by hand.

03 · The build

What I built, piece by piece

Screenshots are from the working demo. Click any image to open it full size.

  1. Step A

    The intake form

    Three fields: email, name and phone. Email and name are required; phone is optional. Short forms get more submissions, and three fields are enough to follow up.

    After submitting, the patient sees a clear confirmation message.

  2. Step B

    A spreadsheet as a simple CRM

    Every submission becomes a row with a timestamp. The clinic gets a running list of inquiries in a tool they already know, without paying for a CRM.

    The test row uses my own email and a dummy phone number.

  3. Step C

    The Make.com scenario

    Six modules in a row. Make.com watches the sheet for new rows, sends the first email, waits, sends the second, waits again, then sends the third. The green checks and “1” badges show each module ran once for the test row.

  4. Step D

    The three emails

    All three arrived in the test inbox, in order.

04 · Testing

The first run failed. Here's how I fixed it.

I handle problems the same way in my day job: record the symptom, find the cause, fix it, then prove the fix works.

Issue 01 · Gmail step Resolved
Symptom
The scenario stopped at the first Gmail step with [403] Request had insufficient authentication scopes
Root cause
The Gmail connection in Make.com didn't have permission to send email from the account.
Fix
Reconnected Gmail in Make.com and granted the send permission.
Verified
Ran the scenario again. It completed with no errors, and all three emails arrived.
05 · For a real clinic

What I'd change before going live

The demo proves the flow works. A live version for a clinic needs four more things:

  • Real gaps between emails In the demo, the waits are a few minutes so the whole sequence can be tested quickly. A clinic would want one to three days, using a “next follow-up date” column and a daily run.
  • Stop once the patient books Add a Status column. When the front desk marks a row as “Booked”, the remaining emails are skipped.
  • The clinic's own email and booking link Send from the clinic's address, with its name, and a link to book an appointment.
  • Careful handling of patient data Collect only what's needed, and limit who can open the sheet.
