01 · The brief

One logo, a family audience, and a lot of nervous patients

Project Smile treats kids, teens and adults. Many patients, children especially, arrive anxious. Most dental websites look either cold and clinical or cheap and cluttered.

The clinic's logo was the only brand asset: dark slate lettering with a single mint sparkle where the “O” becomes a tooth.

The goal: a homepage that makes booking feel easy and unthreatening, and a brand kit so every card, post and message looks like it came from the same clinic.

04 · Brand kit

Rules the clinic can follow without a designer

A guidelines board for logo use, colour, type and icons, plus ready-made templates.

  1. Guidelines

    Logo, colour and type

    Six colours with HEX and RGB codes and a usage ratio, two Google Fonts, logo do's and don'ts, an icon set and button styles.

    The logo's bright mint is kept for small highlights on dark backgrounds only. For text and buttons it is deepened to teal, which reads clearly on white.

  2. Templates

    The brand in use

    Business card front and back, an appointment card, three social posts, a profile icon, an email signature and a Messenger reminder message.

05 · Design decisions

Why it looks the way it does

Colour from the logo, checked for contrast. Teal (#0A6F62) on white scores 5.9:1, above the 4.5:1 accessibility minimum for text.

Two fonts, no more. A rounded, characterful display face for headings and a plain, readable face for everything else.

One spacing scale. Every gap, card padding and section break uses the same 8px steps, so the page feels calm and even.

Photos in frames, never as backgrounds. Images stay sharp and text stays readable at any screen size.

Rounded shapes. Arches and soft corners echo the tooth in the logo and feel friendlier than hard edges.

06 · Before launch

What a live version needs

This is a concept. To go live for a real clinic, it needs four things:

  • Real photos of the clinic and team The images in this concept were generated with Higgsfield AI as stand-ins. Patients should see the actual dentists and rooms.
  • The clinic's own details Text in [brackets] marks content the clinic supplies: hours, address, years open and real Google reviews.
  • A vector logo An SVG or AI file, so the logo and app icon stay crisp in print and on high-resolution screens.
  • Booking that follows up on its own Connect the booking form to the dental follow-up automation I built, so no inquiry goes unanswered.
